# Track: Māori Electorate Vote-Splitting and Upstream List-Seat Secondary Effects

## Status: Completed (2026-11-11)

### Executive Summary
Quantifies the upstream Party Vote (PV) transfer shock and downstream Sainte-Laguë list-seat consequences of tactical vote-splitting in the 7 Māori electorates during the 7 November 2026 General Election.

### Core Discoveries
1. **Party Vote Disparity:** The Green Party secured **45.45% of valid party votes (100,000 votes)** across the 7 Māori electorates, compared to **10.71% (270,000 votes)** in the 64 general electorates.
2. **Tactical Excess Party Votes:** Yielded **+76,429 excess party votes** for the Greens relative to their general-electorate baseline (>50,000 extra votes over 2023).
3. **National Vote Boost:** Inflated the Greens' national party vote share from **11.02% to 13.50% (+2.48 percentage points)**.
4. **Downstream Sainte-Laguë Consequences:**
   - Greens lose **3 list seats** in the counterfactual (dropping from 17 to 14 total seats).
   - National's proportional entitlement rises from 36 to 38 seats, absorbing 2 of its overhang seats (National overhang reduces from 7 to 5 seats; total National seats stay at 43 due to electorate hold).
   - Labour gains **1 list seat** (rising from 31 to 32 total seats).
   - Total Parliament contracts from **131 to 129 seats** (overhang drops from 11 to 9).
5. **Tactical Split-Voting Coordination:**
   - Te Pāti Māori candidate surplus: $\Delta = +41.4\%$ (candidate sweep across all 7 seats).
   - Green Party candidate deficit: $\Delta = -45.5\%$ (full party-vote mobilization without competing candidates).
